# Agent-Pay Domain Model

## Purpose

This document defines the core domain model for Agent-Pay V1. It establishes the boundary between financial authority, spending controls, payment intent, execution, and financial truth.

## Core Principle

> **The Agent should receive spending authority, not financial credentials.**

An Agent does not own user funds and does not directly control payment instruments. Agent-Pay evaluates delegated authority and financial constraints before executing a payment.

## Core Entities

```text
User
 └── Account
      ├── Agents
      ├── Wallets
      ├── Payment Instruments
      └── Funding Sources

Agent
 └── Delegation
      └── Policy / Budget

Agent
 └── Payment Request
      ├── Policy Check
      ├── Budget Check
      ├── Approval
      └── Payment
            └── Transaction
                  └── Ledger
```

### User

Human owner of an Agent-Pay account and the financial authority behind delegated spending.

### Account

Container for financial resources, agents, payment instruments, funding sources, policies, and budgets. The abstraction supports future personal, business, family, or other account models.

### Agent

An AI agent acting on behalf of an Account. An Agent is never the owner of the Account's funds.

### Delegation

A bounded grant of authority from a User/Account to an Agent. Delegation answers what the Agent is authorized to request or perform; it does not itself authorize a particular payment amount.

### Wallet

A logical financial account maintained by Agent-Pay. Wallet balance is derived from the ledger and available balance must account for active holds/reservations.

### Ledger

The financial source of truth. Credits, debits, holds, releases, and adjustments are recorded as immutable financial entries subject to controlled correction mechanisms.

### Payment Instrument

An abstraction over the mechanism used to execute a payment. V1 is designed to support Wallet and future instruments such as Virtual Card, Bank Account, Payment Gateway, or other rails without exposing instrument details to the Agent.

### Funding Source

A source used to fund a wallet or support a payment instrument. It is intentionally separated from the wallet and payment intent models.

### Policy

Rules that determine whether a payment is permitted under conditions such as amount, merchant, category, currency, time, frequency, location, instrument, and approval requirements.

### Budget

A quantitative spending constraint. Policy and Budget are separate: a transaction can satisfy policy rules while still exceeding the available budget.

### Merchant

The counterparty receiving a payment. Merchant identity, trust, and reputation may later integrate with Agentic Trust Foundation without making Agent-Pay responsible for general trust infrastructure.

### Payment Request

The Agent's financial intent. It is a request for execution, not proof that a payment has occurred.

### Approval

A human or policy-driven decision attached to a Payment Request when explicit approval is required. Typical states include `PENDING`, `APPROVED`, `DENIED`, `EXPIRED`, and `CANCELLED`.

### Payment

The execution attempt created after authorization and financial controls have been satisfied. A Payment references the selected instrument and external provider/rail where applicable.

### Transaction

The financial record representing the economic effect of a Payment, including successful payments and later operations such as refunds. Payment and Transaction remain separate concepts.

### Audit Event

An append-oriented record of security, authorization, policy, approval, payment, and administrative actions for traceability and investigation.

### Notification

A user-facing financial event generated by Agent-Pay, such as approval requests, payment completion, denial, or failure.

## Authorization Context

Every payment must retain enough context to explain why the transaction was permitted:

```text
User / Account
      ↓
Agent
      ↓
Delegation
      ↓
Policy
      ↓
Budget
      ↓
Approval (when required)
      ↓
Payment
      ↓
Transaction
      ↓
Ledger
```

Trust and delegation evidence may originate from Agentic Trust Foundation. Financial policy, budget, approval, and payment execution remain Agent-Pay responsibilities.

## Invariants

1. Agents never own user funds.
2. Agents never receive primary financial credentials.
3. Wallet balance is not equivalent to Agent spending authority.
4. Ledger is the source of financial truth.
5. Payment requests are intent; they are not transactions.
6. Payment instruments are abstracted from Agents.
7. Payment operations must support idempotency.
8. Every executed payment has an authorization and financial-control context.
9. Refunds and reversals must produce auditable financial records.
10. Financial state changes must be atomic with their corresponding ledger effects.
